// POLAND — INDUSTRIAL RUINS AND COAST

Energylandia

Zator · PL

An amusement park near Kraków as the first stop — a deliberate counterweight to what came next: concrete and rust. The largest amusement park in Poland.

EC2 — abandoned heating plant

Łódź · PL

Elektrociepłownia EC2 in central Łódź — one of the most photogenic industrial ruins in Poland. Red-brick boiler halls, oversized turbines, chimneys. Part of the complex has been converted to a museum; the rest is still waiting.

The abandoned ship

Gulf of Gdańsk · PL

A cargo ship left where she was laid up, and still furnished: bunks made up, Russian books on the shelves, photographs pinned above a desk, plates left on a mess table. The damage control plan on the bridge names her m/v Khatanga. Down in the engine room the tools still hang in their outlines on the workshop wall; on deck, the sun comes up over the cranes of the port.

Torpedownia — torpedo testing station

Babie Doły · Gdynia · PL

A concrete block standing three hundred metres out in the sea, built on sunken caissons in 1942 as a Luftwaffe torpedo testing station and used until the Germans left in 1945. The Soviets later trained divers here; since then nobody has maintained it and the sea has been taking it apart.

Wolf's Lair

Gierłoż · Kętrzyn · PL

Hitler's field headquarters in the Masurian woods, in use from 1941 to 1944 — and the place where Stauffenberg's bomb went off on 20 July 1944. The briefing had been moved that day out of a reinforced bunker into a light wooden barrack, which is why Hitler walked away from it. Before they left in January 1945 the Germans mined the whole complex; the charges lifted the bunkers and cracked them open without pulverising them, so what stands in the forest today are split concrete blocks the size of houses.

// LITHUANIA — CROSSES, DUNES, SOVIET ARCHITECTURE

Curonian Spit — shifting dunes

Nida · LT · UNESCO

The Curonian Spit is a narrow sandy peninsula separating the Curonian Lagoon from the Baltic Sea. Its migrating sand dunes reach up to 60 metres — the largest in Europe. They drift eastward by a few metres each year and, in the 18th and 19th centuries, swallowed several villages whole.

Hill of Crosses

Šiauliai · LT

A pilgrimage site north of Šiauliai where the faithful have left crosses for centuries. Today there are hundreds of thousands — every size, of wood, metal, stone. The Soviet authorities bulldozed the hill three times; each time, more crosses returned.

Kaunas

Kaunas · LT

Lithuania's second city — an interwar capital, now a living city with brutalist Soviet blocks sitting next to Art Deco and Baroque. The Ninth Fort, a site of mass executions during the Second World War, sits on the outskirts.

// LATVIA — KAROSTA AND THE RIGA CORNER HOUSE

Karosta — abandoned naval base

Liepāja · LV

Karosta (literally „war port") is a district of Liepāja originally built for the Russian Imperial Navy, then used by the Soviet military until 1994. Entire blocks of abandoned barracks, a military hospital, a prison — everything left more or less as the Russians walked away. Part of it is now a museum; the rest decays.

The Corner House — KGB museum

Riga · LV

„Stūra māja" — the Corner House in central Riga, headquarters of the Soviet secret police (NKVD/KGB) from the 1940s to the 1990s. Cells, interrogation rooms, filing cabinets preserved in situ. Today it's a museum about the era of communist repression in Latvia — one of the most affecting in the Baltics.

// ESTONIA — PRISONS AND A WATERFALL

Rummu — submerged prison

Rummu · EE

A limestone quarry with an attached Soviet labour camp. After the quarry was closed in the 1990s, groundwater began to flood the lower levels — today, part of the complex sits underwater. Concrete towers and walls rising from a turquoise lake, a favourite spot for divers and photographers.

Omedu — the kolkhoz roe plant

Omedu · EE

The roe-processing shop of the Kirov fishing kolkhoz, on the western shore of Lake Peipus. A chimney, two enormous tanks and a long low block that looks more like a village school than a factory. Nothing is left inside — whatever the plant once handled is long gone, and the site is now plainly used as an airsoft ground. Vaulted corridors, graffiti, a red eagle painted across a doorway.

Jägala Waterfall

Jägala · EE

Estonia's widest waterfall — over 50 metres across, not particularly tall but imposing in its sheer width and volume. The Jägala river drops over an Ordovician limestone ledge. A break from concrete and rust before the long drive home.

Lennusadam — the Seaplane Harbour

Tallinn · EE

The Estonian Maritime Museum, in the seaplane hangars right on the waterfront. Inside stands the submarine Lembit, built for the Estonian navy by Vickers-Armstrong in 1936 and kept as she was in the 1930s; out at the quay lies the steam icebreaker Suur Tõll, the largest pre-war icebreaker left anywhere. The last stop before the old town, and the only place on this trip where the ships were meant to be boarded.
