// THE GHOST EMPIRE

Yugoslavia dissolved in the nineties, but the infrastructure it left behind didn't disappear — it just stopped being maintained. In the summer of 2020, we drove a loop through six successor states and former satellite countries, following the decay: cooling towers, ideological monuments, military hilltops abandoned when the Cold War ended, and facilities that outlasted the states that built them by thirty years.

// BULGARIA — BUZLUDZHA & SOFIA

BG · Buzludzha · Monument House of the Bulgarian Communist Party

Buzludzha sits at 1441 m on the Balkan ridge, a flying saucer in concrete dropped onto a mountain summit. Built in 1981 as a monument to Bulgarian communism, it was sealed in 1989 when the regime fell. The dome, the mosaic interior with its hammer-and-sickle floors, the propaganda murals — all still there, slowly surrendering to the cold and the damp.

// GREECE — TRAIN GRAVEYARD & THESSALONIKI

GR · Near Thessaloniki · OSE locomotive depot

Just south of Thessaloniki, beside the main rail line, the Greek railway operator OSE left dozens of locomotives and passenger cars to rust in the open. The graveyard grew as the network shrank — austerity-era cutbacks accelerated what time was already doing.

// GREECE — ATHENS: ELLINIKON & THE NATO RADAR

GR · Athens · Ellinikon Olympic Complex / UNHCR site

Athens' old international airport at Ellinikon closed in 2001 when the new airport opened in time for the 2004 Olympics. The runway was repurposed as an Olympic venue — softball, hockey, canoe — and then abandoned again after the games. When the refugee crisis hit in 2015, UNHCR set up a temporary camp in the old terminal buildings and the sports halls.

GR · ACE High NATO Radar Station

The ACE High system was NATO's Cold War troposcatter communications backbone — a chain of relay stations that didn't need satellites or undersea cables. The Greek station, decommissioned when the network was retired in the 1990s, sits on a hilltop with the dish still in place.

// ALBANIA — THE AMMUNITION FACTORY

AL · Poliçan · Uzina e Ndërtimit të Armëve (UNA)

Poliçan was the heart of Albania's Cold War munitions industry — a valley town in the central highlands built entirely around weapons production under Enver Hoxha's regime of enforced self-sufficiency. At its peak, the factory complex employed most of the town. When the regime collapsed in 1991 and the arms contracts with China ended, the factory followed.
