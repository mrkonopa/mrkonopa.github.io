Romania / Summer 2019 / Ridge Traverse

Făgăraș

// ROUTE · AVRIG → VICTORIA · 52.1 KM

// The Long Way South

We left by bus. Romanian newspaper, green hills through the window, the weight of the pack already real before the mountains even appeared. Transylvania looks exactly like you'd imagine it from a train — low white houses with terracotta roofs, wooden fences, hills that keep promising something bigger just behind them.

The Făgăraș massif is the highest part of the Southern Carpathians. Fifty-two kilometres of ridge running roughly east–west above the 2000-metre line, punctuated by glacial lakes and a handful of stone huts that have been there long enough to stop looking temporary. The classic traverse goes Avrig to Victoria — five days if you keep moving, more if the weather turns or you stop minding.

// Day 1 · Into the Forest

The trail out of Avrig climbs hard through Carpathian spruce and beech before the treeline relents. Rocky roots, a red-striped blaze on the bark every hundred metres, nothing dramatic yet. The first hut — Cabana Bârcaciu at 1560 metres — appears like a rumour made solid: red painted wood, yellow-green trim, a cross on the roofline. We had coffee from an enamel mug on a brick ledge outside, shared a Kinder bar, and watched the clouds decide what kind of day they were going to be.

// Days 2–3 · The Ridge

The ridge does not introduce itself gently. You gain the crest and suddenly there is nothing on either side except air and the next peak. On day two the clouds sat low on the cirques — green bowls of alpine meadow, scree, and leftover snow from a winter that hadn't quite let go. The kind of fog that makes the scale of things impossible to read.

Then in the afternoon the clouds broke. The whole massif opened up: layer after layer of peaks running south and east into haze, a small trail marker post standing at the rock edge like a comma. That's the photo that explains why you carry a twelve-kilogram pack for five days.

Snow in July is not unusual up here. Patches of it cling to the north-facing hollows through most of the summer, white interruptions in the green and grey. You cross them quickly — wet rock underneath, good ice axe weather if you slipped — and keep walking.

// Day 4 · Cabana Podragu

Cabana Podragu sits at 2136 metres in a natural bowl below the ridge, built from the same grey limestone the mountain is made of. It looks like it grew there. The hut is one of the highest permanently staffed refuges in Romania — wood smoke, wet boots outside the door, a notebook on the counter where you sign your name. We arrived in rain, the kind that doesn't hurry. The mountains were invisible. We stayed the night anyway.

// Day 5 · 2537

Moldoveanu is the highest point in Romania — 2544 metres, no fanfare, just a cairn and a view that on a clear day reaches across three countries. The altimeter on the watch said 2537 at 15:11. The peak was close enough.

The descent to Victoria is long and the knees notice it. The town itself is post-industrial Transylvania in miniature: apartment blocks, a factory that used to make something important, a train station with a conductor in dress uniform who looks at your ticket like it might be forged. We took the train north.
