ITALY

// GOING SOUTH

In July 2022 we drove a Czech-plated car from Liberec to Sicily and back — thirteen days, four people, one long loop down the spine of Italy to the end of the boot and across the strait onto the island. The plan was deliberately loose: a sequence of locations connected by driving, no fixed hotels until the ferry booking forced it.

Italy has a particular quality of abandonment — the scale of what gets left behind is different from elsewhere. Churches, asylums, seaside resorts, military complexes: all built with an ambition that outlasted whatever it was meant to serve.

// VIA FERRATA · 18 JUL

IT · Dolomites · Via Ferrata

The second day of the drive south, we stopped in the Dolomites for a via ferrata — the fixed-cable route system that allows non-climbers to ascend rock faces that would otherwise require ropes and technical gear. The Dolomite limestone is distinctive: pale orange-grey, sharp-edged, the faces nearly vertical. The via ferrata goes directly up sections that would be unpleasant without the hardware.

At altitude, above the treeline, the scale of the surrounding peaks becomes apparent in a way it isn't from the road. The valleys are deep; the walls on either side are effectively cliffs. You understand why this was serious military terrain in 1915.

Thirty-two photographs from the ascent and the ridge. The camera date was set wrong — all the files say 29 July — but this was day two of the trip.

// OCULUS TOWER · 19 JUL

IT · Northern Italy · Oculus Tower · Abandoned Complex

First dedicated urbex stop: a large derelict complex in northern Italy near Ferrara (GPS 44.84°N, 11.59°E) known in the urbex community as the Oculus Tower — named for the circular aperture at the top of the main tower that draws a column of daylight straight down through the interior. The structure is industrial or institutional in origin, now completely empty.

The light effect is genuine architecture — intentional or not, it produces something that recalls a precedent from antiquity. The surrounding complex added its own corridors, collapsed roofing, and overgrown courtyards. Thirty-seven frames from this site: most of them looking upward.

// ABANDONED SEASIDE RESORT · 20 JUL

IT · Southern Italy · Derelict Holiday Complex

Somewhere in the southern half of the country, set back from the coast, was a holiday complex that had closed a long time ago. The pools were still in place — empty, cracked, the plaster going the colour of old concrete. Cabanas along one side, a main building with a reception area that still had the desk. The kind of place that operated for two or three seasons in the seventies, then didn't.

In high Italian summer heat, abandoned water infrastructure has a specific sadness to it. The engineering was all still correct — drains, filter housing, pump connections — just without water, without people, without the context that made any of it make sense.

// CRACO · 21 JUL

IT · Basilicata · Craco · Ghost Town

Craco is a medieval hilltop town in Basilicata that was evacuated in 1963 following a landslide and never reoccupied. The buildings are still there — the church, the castle tower, the streets and stairways — just completely empty, and steadily losing the battle with the hillside.

It's legitimately eerie in a way that most abandoned places aren't. Most abandoned buildings feel like they were just stopped; Craco feels like it was interrupted. The street layout implies the life that used to happen in it — the routes between houses, the public square, the logic of daily movement — and all of it is still physically present, just without anyone to use it.

The views from the upper part of the town are significant: the Basento valley spread below, the pale Basilicata light, the colours of the stone matching the colours of the landscape in a way that makes the town look grown rather than built.

// SICILY — ETNA · 22 JUL

IT · Sicily · Etna · 3357 m

We crossed into Sicily on the Messina ferry — the mainland to island crossing takes twenty minutes and costs almost nothing — and drove northeast to Etna. The summit area is accessible by road to around 2000 metres; above that, on foot. The volcanic landscape is unlike anything else: black lava fields, solidified mid-flow, vegetation growing back through the rock in the older sections. Craters in various states of geological youth.

Eleven frames from Etna, which undercounts the experience. Some places are hard to photograph because what's striking about them is the scale rather than any particular view.

// AGRIGENTO + PALERMO · 23 JUL

IT · Sicily · Agrigento · Valley of the Temples

The Valley of the Temples at Agrigento is a UNESCO site containing some of the best-preserved Doric temples outside Greece — built between the 6th and 5th centuries BC, when Akragas was one of the wealthiest cities in the ancient Mediterranean. They're not in a valley at all but on a ridge, visible from a long distance, which presumably was the point.

The Temple of Concordia is the best preserved. It survived because it was converted into a Christian church in the 6th century and maintained continuously for another thousand years. The columns are still standing because someone always needed them to be.

IT · Sicily · Palermo · Ferry Port

Palermo in the evening, heading for the port. The city moves at a different pace from the north — narrower streets, louder, the architecture a layering of Arab-Norman and Baroque that produces something unlike anywhere else in Italy. We didn't have time for it that evening. The ferry was boarding.

// PALERMO → SALERNO FERRY · 24 JUL

IT · Tyrrhenian Sea · GNV Night Ferry

The GNV overnight ferry from Palermo to Salerno takes around ten hours. Two photographs from the crossing: the ship leaving port and the coast resolving out of the morning haze on the other side. The Tyrrhenian in July is flat enough that the crossing is unremarkable as sailing goes. The point is to wake up on the other side.

// AMALFI COAST · 25 JUL

IT · Campania · Vietri sul Mare · Amalfi Coast

The Amalfi Coast is the section of Campania coastline between Salerno and Positano: cliffs dropping directly to the sea, villages built vertically into the rock, a single road cut into the cliff face that alternates between tunnel and open drop depending on the metre. In summer it's extremely busy and extremely beautiful — two facts that coexist without resolving.

Vietri sul Mare at the eastern end of the coast is known for its ceramics — the hand-painted tilework that covers the church dome and decorates the shopfronts. We walked it and moved on, aware that the next day had a long programme.

// NATO BASE PROTO · 26 JUL

IT · Campania · Cold War NATO Underground Facility

This was the main event of the trip — a decommissioned NATO facility built into a hillside in Campania during the Cold War, intended to function as a hardened command-and-control installation capable of surviving a nuclear event nearby. The construction involved tunnelling directly into the rock: kilometres of corridors, blast doors at intervals, equipment rooms off the main passages, ventilation shafts going up through the hillside to the surface.

Ninety-six photographs from inside. The scale demands it — the corridor lengths alone justify stopping every fifty metres. The installation is in the particular state of abandonment where nothing has been cleared out but nothing functions: racks of communications equipment, cable runs still in their conduit clips, indicator panels with the bulbs gone but the labels still readable. The blast doors are impressive objects in their own right — steel, counterweighted, three hundred millimetres of closure tolerances still holding after forty years of no maintenance.

Cold War infrastructure in Italy is less documented than equivalent sites in Germany or the UK. The facility is not well-known outside specialist circles. The hillside above it looks completely normal — farmland, scrub, nothing that marks the volume of engineered space beneath.

// ROME · 27 JUL

IT · Lazio · Rome · Basilica San Giovanni in Laterano · Imperial Forum

Rome after the NATO base felt like a shift in register. The Basilica of San Giovanni in Laterano — the cathedral of Rome, technically outranking St Peter's in ecclesiastical hierarchy — has an interior that operates at a different scale from anything built in the last two centuries. The nave proportions, the Cosmatesque floor, the baldachin above the main altar. Baroque ambition expressed through Gothic space.

The Imperial Forum area — the sequence of forums built by Caesar, Augustus, Nerva, Trajan — is still partially visible from street level, the excavated sections lower than the modern city, the columns standing at their original height above a grade that has risen two metres since they were placed. The Forum of Nerva is particularly strange: two Corinthian columns still standing between apartment buildings, a frieze of Minerva carved into the wall above them, the surrounding forum entirely gone.

// ABANDONED CINEMA · 28 JUL

IT · Central Italy · Abandoned Cinema

On the drive north from Rome, we stopped at an abandoned cinema — a large derelict theatre somewhere in central Italy, its auditorium intact but stripped, the projection booth still accessible, the screen frame still standing. The kind of building that closed somewhere in the eighties or nineties and was never repurposed, just left.

The acoustics of an empty auditorium are specific. Thirty-three photographs from the interior — the seating rake, the stage area, the ceiling details that survive long after everything else has gone.

// LAGO D'ISEO · 29 JUL

IT · Lombardy · Lago d'Iseo · Hans Nalejvárna

The final stop before the drive home: a small lakeside bar on Lago d'Iseo that has become a tradition on Italy trips. The lake sits in a pre-Alpine basin west of Brescia, surrounded by steep wooded slopes, one of the less-visited of the northern lakes. Hans serves cold beer. After thirteen days on the road, that is exactly what is needed.

From there: north through Austria, back into the Czech Republic, Liberec by late evening.
